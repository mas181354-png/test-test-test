# 30-Second Video Storyboard — Samsung-Compatible Replacement TV Remote

**Product:** Universal replacement remote control compatible with Samsung Smart TVs (BN59-01315J style layout, with streaming app shortcut keys)
**Placement:** Amazon Sponsored Brands video / Brand Ads
**Duration:** 30 seconds
**Master aspect ratio:** 16:9, 1920×1080
**Core idea:** *"The tired-at-the-end-of-the-day moment — one remote that just works."*

---

## 1. Strategy in one paragraph

The buyer for this product is not shopping for a gadget — they are shopping to **fix an annoyance**. Their original remote is lost, cracked, sticky, or the buttons stopped responding. So the ad does not open with a feature list; it opens with the **feeling of coming home tired and wanting the TV to just turn on**. The product is the resolution of that tension, not the subject of it. Three or four concrete features are then delivered fast, in macro, while the emotional payoff plays out in the background.

Amazon video ads autoplay **muted** in most placements, so every claim is carried by on-screen text first and voiceover second. The product is on screen in frame one and never leaves for more than three seconds.

**Casting:** Warm female voice, 30s–40s, UK English (this listing is amazon.co.uk). Conversational and unhurried — not an announcer. Record a US English alternate if you plan to run on amazon.com.

## 5. Audio & music

- **Music bed:** Warm, minimal, mid-tempo indie-electronic. Soft piano or plucked synth motif over a low pad. Must be **royalty-free and licensed for paid advertising** — Amazon requires you to hold rights to every asset.
- **Structure:** low pad (0:00–0:10) → drop out briefly at 0:09.5 → rhythmic pulse enters at 0:10 for the feature block → resolves warmly at 0:26.
- **SFX:** key clicks (shots 1, 4, 7), door and keys (shot 2), cushion settle (shot 3), TV power-on chime (shot 5), signal whoosh (shot 6).
- **Mix:** Voiceover clearly above the bed. Target roughly **−16 LUFS integrated**, true peak **≤ −2 dBTP**, stereo, 48 kHz. Audio must be present throughout — a silent track fails Amazon's checks.
- **Captions:** Burn in captions or ensure the on-screen text carries the whole message. Assume muted playback.

---

## 6. On-screen text & typography

- **Typeface:** A clean geometric sans — Inter, Poppins, or Montserrat. Bold for headlines, Medium for sub-lines.
- **Headline size:** ~72–84px at 1080p. **Sub-line:** ~40–48px.
- **Colour:** White with a subtle drop shadow or a 60%-opacity dark scrim behind it. Add one accent colour drawn from your own brand — avoid pure Samsung blue, which reads as an official Samsung ad.
- **Safe area:** Keep all text inside the central 90% of the frame. Amazon overlays its own UI chrome near the edges in several placements.
- **Timing:** Every text card on screen a minimum of **2 seconds**. Never more than two lines at once.
- **Motion:** Simple 8-frame fade-up plus a 10px rise. No spins, no bounces.

## 7. Amazon ad-policy checklist

Verify against the current Amazon Ads creative acceptance policy before submitting — these are the items this creative is built around:

- [x] 16:9, 1920×1080, H.264 MP4, ~8 Mbps or higher
- [x] Duration inside the accepted range (6–45s; 30s sits in the recommended zone)
- [x] No blank, black, or white frame at the start or end
- [x] Product visible in the opening frames and in the end card
- [x] Works fully muted — all claims carried by on-screen text
- [x] All text inside the central 90% safe area
- [x] No Amazon logo, Amazon name, or Amazon-owned marks (including "Amazon's Choice" and "Best Seller" badges)
- [x] No prices, no discounts, no "#1", no star ratings, no quoted customer reviews
- [x] No "click here" / cursor-based calls to action
- [x] No recognisable third-party content on the TV screen — abstract glow only
- [x] Described as *compatible with* Samsung, never as an official or genuine Samsung product
- [x] No Samsung logo or Samsung brand styling in overlay graphics
- [x] Third-party streaming logos appear only as printed on the physical product, never as overlay graphics or animated lockups
- [x] Music and all assets cleared for commercial advertising use
- [ ] **Your action:** confirm the model-compatibility claim in shot 6 matches the compatibility list in your live listing, word for word

> **Flag worth taking seriously:** the reference photo from the listing uses blue dashed highlight boxes around the streaming logos. That treatment is fine as a static listing image but is riskier in video, where animated emphasis on third-party marks can read as implied endorsement. The board deliberately keeps those logos as incidental product detail instead.

## 9. Two alternate hooks worth A/B testing

The first three seconds do most of the work. Both alternates keep everything from shot 3 onward identical.

**Hook B — "The broken one":** 0:00–0:03, macro of a battered original remote with a cracked case and a dead, unresponsive button being jabbed repeatedly. Hard cut to the new remote. Text: `WHEN THE OLD ONE GIVES UP.` Speaks directly to replacement intent.

**Hook C — "The hunt":** 0:00–0:03, fast handheld shots of hands digging under sofa cushions, in a drawer, behind pillows. Cut to the new remote sitting cleanly on the table. Text: `STOP HUNTING FOR IT.` Highest relatability, best for a cold audience.

## 10. Production notes

The frames in this board are AI-generated **pre-visualisation** — they exist to lock composition, lighting and lens choice before you shoot or brief anyone.

For the finished ad, the four product-centric shots (1, 4, 6, 7) should use **real footage or photography of the actual unit you ship**. Amazon requires the creative to accurately represent the product, and generated imagery will not reproduce the exact button layout, printed labels or model number reliably. The button geometry in these frames is approximate.

- **Shots 1, 6, 7** are achievable as a half-day tabletop shoot: one light, one reflector, a macro lens and a turntable. Shot 6 can be a composite of a single product shot over a studio background.
- **Shots 2, 3, 5, 8** need a location, one actor and about half a day. Shoot them in continuity in a single room so the light matches.
- **Shot 4** is a macro insert of a real hand on the real product. Do not fake this one — thumb-on-button is the shot that carries the product's tactility.
- If the budget is tight: shoot 1, 4, 6, 7 for real and use lifestyle stock for 2, 3, 5, 8. The product must be genuine; the living room does not have to be.

The exact generation prompts for every frame are in [`frame-prompts.md`](./frame-prompts.md) so any frame can be re-rolled or re-styled.
